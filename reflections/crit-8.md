# Crit 8 reflection — It's alive!

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The biggest breakthrough was finally settling on the core idea. At the beginning, I had very little inspiration, so I spent a lot of time looking through different interactive demos and brainstorming with Codex before I eventually arrived at a theme that felt worth developing.

After deciding on the direction, I used the `grillme` skill to challenge and refine the idea from different angles. It helped me think through the purpose of the product, the interaction model, what should deliberately be left out, and how the different design decisions should fit together.

Because of this, I feel that the project started with a relatively solid foundation. Instead of immediately asking an agent to build features, I first spent time making the idea itself clearer and more coherent, which made the later implementation much easier to guide.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to become a software developer who has ideas, creativity, and a desire to build products that can provide some value to society.

The idea for this project came partly from my own experience. In the past, when I was dealing with a lot of anxiety and depression, a psychologist suggested that I write down the things that were troubling me instead of keeping them constantly in my head. I found that this genuinely helped me feel lighter.

That experience became the starting point for Throwaway. I wanted to take that simple idea further and create a shared space where people could externalise something they had been carrying and, in some small way, feel that they had put it down.

This project also made me realise that the kind of developer I want to become is not defined only by technical ability. I also want to bring human experience, empathy and personal judgement into the products I build. AI can help me generate code, explore alternatives and implement ideas, but the reason a product should exist, the experience it should create, and the human value behind it still need to come from people.
