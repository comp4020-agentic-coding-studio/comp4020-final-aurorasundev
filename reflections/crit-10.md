# Crit 10 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was finding the right case study for Claude to work from. When I first described my Gallery idea, the result worked, but it looked like a rough template. Asking Claude to find examples on its own did not give us a clear visual direction either. While browsing, I found a case that showed both the finished interaction and an explanation of how it was built. That gave me something concrete to point to: the composition, the way objects moved, and the technical approach behind them.

I could then tell Claude which parts suited Throwaway and add my own rules for the records, paper sleeves and opening animation. My feedback became more specific, and the Gallery started to feel closer to what I had imagined. Finding a useful reference mattered as much as writing another prompt.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to be a developer who understands the technology and design principles behind what I am building. Claude can reduce the amount of work I do, but I still need enough knowledge to direct it and judge the result.

That includes learning the vocabulary of web design and animation. If I say “make some objects move around a centre,” there is room for many interpretations. If I describe an orbital Gallery, then specify how it rotates, brings one work into focus and settles into place, Claude has a much clearer task. I think this can save rounds of explanation. More importantly, it helps me remain responsible for the design instead of accepting the first version that works.
